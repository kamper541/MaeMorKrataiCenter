# Decision D10: Deleting a Category Leaves Its Items Uncategorized

- **Date**: 2026-11-21
- **Spec**: [SPEC-002](../specs/SPEC-002-master-data-to-backend.md)
- **Status**: APPROVED

## 1. Context & Problem Statement
The round-2 Spectre audit found that the `love` category could be deleted once its suggestions were gone, leaving love situations (which are built from love statuses) under a category that no longer exists. Options were to protect `love` from deletion, or to define what happens to a deleted category's items.

## 2. Decision
- Any category can be deleted, including `love`. This replaces the earlier rule (D09 / SPEC-002) that a category with situations or suggestions returns `409`.
- In the same atomic batch, the category's situations and suggestions get `category = null` ("uncategorized"). There is no `uncategorized` category row.
- If `love` is deleted, the love statuses are still love statuses (cards keep their texts), but the love situations derived from them become uncategorized.
- Uncategorized items are **shown** in the public reading config, in separate `uncategorized_situations` and `uncategorized_suggestions` lists.
- Admins can reassign an uncategorized situation or suggestion to a category with PUT, or create one with `category: null`. Creating a category `love` again turns the love statuses back into love situations.
- Each moved situation and suggestion gets its own `audit_events` row (`update`, changed field `category`), in addition to the category's `delete` row.

## 3. Rationale
- **Security (#1)**: No impact; same admin-only writes, still audited.
- **Technical Debt (#2)**: No reserved special category to protect; a nullable column is simple and explicit.
- **Speed (#3)**: Deleting a category is a single call.

## 4. Consequences
- `category` is nullable on situations and suggestions in the schema and in admin writes.
- The public `ReadingConfig` gains two arrays; frontend must decide how to display them.
- Deleting a category never returns `409`.
